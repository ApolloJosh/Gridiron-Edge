# Week 1, 2026 — the first live grade

The first genuinely out-of-sample test: projections built entirely from 2025 data, scored
against what actually happened. 227 players qualified, **188 recorded a line**.

## Headline numbers

| | MAE | bias | band coverage | corr(proj, actual) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All (n=188) | 32.3 | **+0.6** | **46.8%** | 0.80 |
| QB (n=28) | 71.0 | −3.1 | 46.4% | −0.18 |
| RB (n=47) | 25.4 | −1.7 | 51.1% | 0.54 |
| WR/TE (n=113) | 25.7 | +2.5 | 45.1% | 0.35 |

**The range held up.** 46.8% of actual results landed inside the 25–75 band, against an ideal
of 50% and a backtested 47.7%. The distribution is the part of this tool that works, and it
worked on data it had never seen.

**Overall bias was +0.6 yards** — essentially unbiased in aggregate.

**Quarterback projections were worthless.** Correlation between projection and actual was
**−0.18**. Small sample (28), but combined with a 71-yard MAE it means the QB board carried no
information in Week 1. Cross-season QB projection is the hardest case: new teams, new
coordinators, and the largest offseason roster churn of any position.

## Three failures worth fixing

### 1. Elite baselines overshot by +12.8 yards

The top EDGE quartile's projections ran **+12.8 yards high**. Nothing in the model accounted
for regression: a player's 2025 line is partly true talent and partly the high end of a noisy
17-game sample, and the second half doesn't repeat.

Fix: shrink each baseline toward the positional mean. Validated two independent ways before
shipping — a within-2025 holdout and Week 1 itself:

| | 2025 holdout best k | Week 1 best k | shipped (cross-season) |
|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 0.90 (n=371) | 0.60 (n=28) | **0.80** |
| WR/TE | 0.85 (n=1970) | 0.70 (n=113) | **0.82** |
| RB | **1.00** (n=889) | **1.00** (n=47) | **0.97** |

Both datasets agree on direction and on the exception: **running backs do not want shrinkage.**
Week 1 wanted more aggressive shrinkage than the within-season holdout, which is what you'd
expect crossing an offseason, so the shipped values sit between the two rather than chasing the
smaller sample. Within-season values (0.90 / 0.85 / 1.00) apply once the current year has data.

### 2. 17% of projected players never played

39 of 227 didn't record a line. The worst cases were quarterbacks: **Tua Tagovailoa was on the
board at EDGE 64 while sitting behind Michael Penix Jr.** Mac Jones (SF) and Cooper Rush were
ranked the same way. The tool had no concept of a starter.

Fix: ESPN's core API exposes depth charts at
`seasons/{year}/teams/{id}/depthcharts` — three charts per team, the offensive one identified by
carrying a `qb` position group (its name varies: "3WR 1TE", "2WR 2TE"). Gating the board at
QB1 / RB1-3 / WR1-4 / TE1-2 cut the miss rate **17.2% → 9.0%**. Depth charts lag reality, so
it's a default-on toggle rather than a hard exclusion, and players with no chart entry pass through.

### 3. The opponent term still isn't paying for itself

Consistent with the season backtest, the opponent adjustment added nothing in Week 1 — it made
MAE slightly *worse* at every position. Its weight in the EDGE score drops from 40% to 15%.

## A correction to the first reading of this data

The initial pass looked damning: top EDGE quartile averaged 0.87× their own baseline with only
31.9% beating it, bottom quartile 1.22× and 53.2%. That reads as a ranking that is actively
inverted.

**It mostly isn't.** Re-running the comparison at *zero* opponent weight leaves the spread at
−0.242, barely different from −0.243 at 15% and not far from −0.348 at 40%. And
`corr(softness, act/base)` is only **−0.048**. The opponent term is not what produces the effect.

The effect is largely an artifact of the metric. `actual / baseline` is unbounded above and
floored at zero: a receiver averaging 20 yards can post 3× his average, one averaging 100
cannot. Ranking by quality and then measuring a ratio will always look inverted.

Judged properly, the ranking does carry signal: **corr(EDGE, actual production) = +0.242**, and
high-EDGE players produced more in absolute terms. The real defect was overshoot on elite
players — a bias problem, which shrinkage addresses — not an inverted ranking.

The report card in the app now reports **median miss** per quartile instead of a ratio, for
exactly this reason.

Week 2 as the clean test.

## The real change: it grades itself now

Every scan snapshots its projections to local storage. Once a week's games are final, the
**Report Card** view scores them — MAE and bias by position, band coverage, miss rate, and
whether the top EDGE quartile actually outperformed the bottom.

One week is 188 data points and proves very little on its own. Ten weeks is a real sample, and
now it accumulates without anyone remembering to collect it.
